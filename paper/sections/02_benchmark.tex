\section{Minimal Benchmark}

An episode contains \ResultMerchantCount{} merchants in a fixed external order.
Querying merchant $t$ consumes an integer resource vector
$c_t=(c_t^{\mathrm{time}},c_t^{\mathrm{token}},c_t^{\mathrm{call}},
c_t^{\mathrm{spend}})$ and reveals either unavailability or one landed price.
Available offers are ephemeral: an offer that is not accepted immediately
cannot be recalled.  Exhausting the merchant sequence or losing query
feasibility without purchasing incurs the registered failure penalty.

The primary benchmark records discrete merchant-specific price distributions,
stockout mass, and query requirements.  It evaluates
\ResultHardConstraintScenarioCount{} declared operating regimes on the same
forecasts and the same observed offer of \$\ResultObservedPrice{}.  The regimes
include relaxed resources, individually binding time, token, API-call, and
API-spend limits, a combined limit, and a purchase-price cap of
\$\ResultPriceCappedLimit{}.  The complete inputs are generated in
\texttt{artifacts/results.json}; they are synthetic benchmark values rather
than market estimates.

The primary outcome is the exact action---buy, continue, or reject without a
feasible next query---together with the reservation price and remaining budget.
Simpler stopping and routing policies are retained as controls, but every
deployable policy must satisfy the same hard feasibility checks.
