\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The central question is whether one more merchant inspection earns its cost. We
provide a replayable costly-search implementation that answers it with mathematics
rather than an always-search shopping heuristic. Shopify seller decks quantify the value of
another reveal inside an observed graph; UCP direct-merchant panels characterize the
wider search surface that an agent can choose to enter.

The registered held-out result is that adaptive stopping reliably beats costed
search-all on the UCP panel at every declared real-dollar scenario and on Shopify at
the long-horizon cost. At zero cost search-all is reliably better; at the two small
positive-cost sensitivity levels, the policies are not reliably separated.
That is actionable: a shopping agent should keep searching only while the prospective
saving repays the next inspection, rather than assuming that every seller must be
opened.
