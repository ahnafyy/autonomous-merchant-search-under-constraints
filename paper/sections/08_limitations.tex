\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{One catalog-search ecosystem, two dates.}
The corpus covers merchants discovered through one agentic-commerce capability
standard and sampled on two days a month apart. Nothing here establishes that these
merchants represent retail commerce, and two snapshots cannot resolve intra-month
dynamics. A price that fell and recovered between our dates is recorded as unchanged.
Daily panel observations and weekly discovery runs are ongoing, but are outside the
evidence release evaluated here.

\paragraph{The effect is small enough to be fragile.}
The live-panel improvement is a fraction of a percent of purchase cost. The interval
excludes zero, but an effect of this size is more sensitive to episode selection than
a large one would be. We would not be surprised if a differently filtered corpus
moved it materially, and we would be surprised if it reversed.

\paragraph{Thin merchant overlap.}
Almost every shared SKU is carried by exactly two merchants. This bounds what any
stopping rule can demonstrate, prevents the routing and nonbinding-budget controls
described in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}, and means the live result does not
distinguish the full dynamic program from one-step lookahead.

\paragraph{The currency filter discards most candidates.}
Requiring a single currency removes the majority of cross-merchant SKUs. Admitting
them would need an exchange-rate assumption at a fixed date, which we judged worse
than a smaller corpus. The surviving episodes span only two currencies and may not be
representative of the ones we dropped.

\paragraph{Catalog offers are not checkout outcomes.}
We treat presence in a merchant's catalog as availability and absence as a delisting,
and only for merchants whose catalogs were paginated in full. No checkout was
attempted, so a listed item may not have been purchasable at the displayed catalog
price. Re-querying a merchant is technically possible; the primary model instead
assumes that continuing does not reserve the earlier catalog offer. Rate limits are
assumed rather than published.

\paragraph{The declared-capability assumption.}
Merchants are included because they advertise the read operation we exercised. This
is a documented modelling assumption, not case-by-case permission review or explicit
consent, and it is the weakest link in the collection methodology.

\paragraph{The sweep is simulation.}
The decision table comes from a generator calibrated to measured churn and dispersion
but is not a measurement. It draws merchant prices independently, whereas real
merchants may move together in response to shared costs, which would compress the
dispersion that drives the entire advantage. Its thresholds are grid boundaries, so
the true boundary lies somewhere between adjacent levels.

\paragraph{Permit safety is a conjecture.}
We observe no budget violations across every replayed episode, which is evidence and
not proof. The property is registered as a conjecture, and it does not hold when an
upstream service can spend beyond an issued permit.

\paragraph{No cross-episode learning.}
Forecasts are frozen from the calibration snapshot. An agent that learned across
episodes might do better, or might overfit to merchants that disappear; we did not
test it.
