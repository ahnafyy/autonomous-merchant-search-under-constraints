No substantive result is claimed in the current release.  The finite action maps
generated by the implementation are validation fixtures: showing that a policy
reacts to binding constraints is expected behavior.

The Python reservation ledger passes focused implementation checks for atomic
failure, exact reclamation, conservative censored charging, and one-time
reconciliation.  These checks do not prove the pathwise statement registered as

\texttt{MERCHANT-PERMIT-SAFETY-CONJECTURE-001}.  The joint-action solver and its
comparison with exhaustive enumeration are also not implemented, so
\texttt{MERCHANT-PERMIT-SOLVER-OPEN-001} remains open.

The empirical question is whether joint adaptive merchant and permit
selection improves a preregistered purchase outcome over the strongest fixed
feasible baseline while retaining zero hard-budget violations.  A future result
must use frozen paired held-out merchant panels, include planner overhead, timeouts,
truncation, and forecast error, and report constrained, nonbinding-budget, and
exhaustive-oracle comparisons separately.  Any total-budget violation is a
correctness failure rather than an objective tradeoff.

Until those standards are met and the closest safe-inference and online-allocation
literature is resolved, claim \texttt{MERCHANT-PERMIT-OPEN-001} remains \emph{open}.
