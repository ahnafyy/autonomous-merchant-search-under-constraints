\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A shopping agent faces a simple operational question after every useful result: buy
now, or pay to search once more? Another merchant inspection can lower the eventual
price, but it consumes time, calls, money, and user attention. The paper is about
whether that next search action is worth its cost.

We answer that question with a mathematical decision rule and two complementary
datasets. Contemporaneous seller decks from a frozen Shopify Global Catalog collection
provide competing offers inside an observed product graph, allowing a held-out
comparison of adaptive stopping and costed search-all. A UCP direct-merchant panel series
measures the broader catalog surface an agent may choose to search beyond that graph.
The implementation retains the best observed seller card; this state detail isolates
the marginal value of another inspection. Together, the sources turn ``keep looking''
from a heuristic into a measurable economic action. They are analyzed separately
because they answer different parts of the same decision.

\paragraph{What this release establishes.}
The Shopify collection yields replayable single-currency seller decks; this is a
descriptive numerical claim, not an estimate of all retail sellers.

The UCP quality analysis records and excludes common-mode retrieval discontinuities
instead of reclassifying them as offer disappearance.

The UCP panel favors adaptive stopping over costed search-all in every registered
real-dollar scenario; the smaller Shopify replay favors it at the long-horizon cost
and is inconclusive at the two lower costs.

\paragraph{Why two sources matter.}
The sources expose the two levers an agent must price. Shopify shows the value of
looking among competing sellers already visible for a product. UCP shows that reaching
outside an observed graph is itself an inspection problem, with collection and
validation cost. The same mathematics governs both: continue only when the expected
improvement exceeds the next search cost. This makes the rule useful to an agent that
searches inside an aggregator, expands beyond it, or does both in sequence.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Every numerical value is generated from collected data and configured analysis.
The study asks whether adaptive stopping earns its complexity by avoiding inspections
that cost more than their expected saving. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states the
boundaries of that conclusion.
