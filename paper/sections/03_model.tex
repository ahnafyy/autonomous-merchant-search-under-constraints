\section{The Search-or-Stop Decision}
\label{sec:model}

For a product deck, seller card $i$ has a price $P_i$ revealed only when inspected.
After any inspection the agent retains the cheapest observed card as its incumbent.
Its state is

\begin{equation}
  (p^\star, S, b),
\end{equation}

where $p^\star$ is the best retained price, $S$ is the set of unopened cards, and
$b$ is remaining inspection budget. The action is to buy $p^\star$ or inspect a
feasible card. Recall is free within this model: buying a retained card does not
require a second price draw or revalidation.

Inspection must appear in the objective. With free recall and zero opening cost,
opening every feasible card weakly improves the retained best price, leaving no
meaningful stopping decision. We therefore score a replay by retained item price
plus a declared inspection cost. The reported USD rate-card scenarios and their
market-rate multipliers are sensitivity analysis, not observed provider billing.

For a price-minimization Pandora model with scalar inspection cost $c_i$, a
reservation price $z_i$ satisfies

\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[(z_i-P_i)^+] = c_i.
  \label{eq:pandora-reservation}
\end{equation}

The comparison policy opens another card only when its estimated improvement over
$p^\star$ exceeds its cost; the dynamic policy uses calibration decks to estimate
the same search-or-stop decision. This follows the reservation-value structure of
costly inspection, rather than irreversible selection rules
~\citep{weitzman1979optimal,boodaghians2020pandora}.

\subsection{Replay protocol}

The product-level split prevents a policy from calibrating on the seller prices of a
held-out title. The reported online control, search-all, reveals every card and pays
every inspection cost. Adaptive stopping sees the same replay order and can stop after
any reveal; it retains the cheapest card observed so far. Held-out cards are never
used by either policy until the replay reveals them.

The model is intentionally narrower than commerce. It assumes retained catalog
offers can be purchased at their observed price and omits shipping, tax, stock
changes, checkout, and revalidation. Those are state transitions for a future model,
not evidence supplied by the present data.
