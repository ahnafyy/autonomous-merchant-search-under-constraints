\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Weitzman's costly-search model supplies the reservation-value intuition: pay to
inspect an alternative and retain the best observed value~\citep{weitzman1979optimal}.
Pandora variants study order constraints, deadlines, and online presentation
~\citep{boodaghians2020pandora,berger2024pandora,esfandiari2019online}. Our work does
not claim a new Pandora theorem. It adapts the recalled-card state to a shopping
replay and reports the data limitations that prevent a comparative performance claim.

Consumer search also studies price discovery when price distributions are uncertain
~\citep{rothschild1974searching}. The empirical distinction here is that policies are
calibrated on a disjoint set of product decks rather than assumed to know a universal
price distribution.

Irrevocable secretary and no-recall stopping results answer a different question:
they reject an observed option before seeing later ones. They should not be used to
model a shopping agent that can return to its retained card. Conversely, the current
free-recall replay does not establish the persistence or revalidation conditions that
would make recall appropriate at checkout.
