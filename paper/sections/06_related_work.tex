\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

The stopping rule used here is not new, and this section places it rather than
defends it. Records for every work cited below, including the date each DOI was
checked, are kept in \texttt{research/literature.yml}.

\paragraph{Search with a reservation price.}
The idea that a searcher should accept any offer below a threshold determined by the
value of continuing goes back to consumer search theory, including the case where the
price distribution itself is unknown~\citep{rothschild1974searching}, and to
Weitzman's analysis of which alternative to inspect next~\citep{weitzman1979optimal}.
Our recurrence is of this family. The differences are operational rather than
conceptual: the searcher faces a vector of hard consumable resources, an offer may be
unavailable rather than merely expensive, and ending without a purchase carries an
explicit penalty.

\paragraph{Costly inspection.}
The Pandora's box literature studies paying to reveal a value before deciding whether
to take it. Recent variants add order constraints on which box may be opened
next~\citep{boodaghians2020pandora}, deadlines~\citep{berger2024pandora}, values that
change over time~\citep{amanatidis2026pandora}, and online arrival of the boxes
themselves~\citep{esfandiari2019online}. Our setting shares the costly-inspection
structure and the commitment requirement. It differs in that the inspection cost is a
vector of enforceable limits reserved before the call rather than a scalar price, and
in that inspection can fail to return a purchasable offer at all.

\paragraph{The recursion we use.}
The finite-horizon threshold sequence in Section~\ref{sec:rule} is the Cayley--Moser
problem: full-information sequential selection from i.i.d. offers without recall,
posed by Cayley in 1875, solved by Moser in 1956, and analysed at length
by~\citet{gilbert1966recognizing}. \citet{ferguson1989secretary} gives the attribution
history and separates it from the rank-only secretary problem it is often confused
with. Its asymptotics are known to high order~\citep{finch2024quadratic,
entwistle2022asymptotics}, and it remains under study in continuous
time~\citep{katriel2025cayley}. We reproduce it in a price-minimization
parameterization and claim nothing for the recursion itself.

\paragraph{Irrevocable selection.}
Secretary and prophet models study accepting or rejecting irrevocably under
uncertainty, including sampling costs~\citep{lorenzen1981optimal} and random arrival
order~\citep{esfandiari2017prophet}. Our no-recall assumption matches theirs, and the
explore-then-accept baseline we compare against is the classic secretary rule. That
rank-based rules are suboptimal under a cardinal objective is
established: \citet{bearden2006secretary} shows the optimal cutoff is not $n/e$ once
payoffs are cardinal, and \citet{palley2014sequential} separates full-information from
rank-only sequential search theoretically and experimentally. Our contribution there
is to measure the size of the penalty in this setting, not to demonstrate the
mismatch.

\paragraph{Online decisions under resource limits.}
Online packing and allocation with capacity constraints in the random-order
model~\citep{kesselheim2014primal}, and online search with known price
bounds~\citep{elyaniv2001optimal}, are the closest operational analogues:
irreversible actions taken against limited resources. Their guarantees rest on
assumptions our data does not satisfy, but the framing of resources as state rather
than as a cost term is the one we adopt.

\paragraph{What is different here.}
The contribution is not the algorithm. It is a measurement on a live merchant
ecosystem, and a mapping of the conditions under which this well-known rule is and is
not worth deploying. We are not aware of comparable published measurements of
cross-merchant price dispersion and listing churn for protocol-enabled autonomous
shopping, which is exactly the input any of the models above would need before being
applied. Because the priority full texts have not been read line by line, we make no
priority or novelty claim over the works cited, and the literature records remain
marked as candidates.
