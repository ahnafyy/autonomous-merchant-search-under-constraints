\section{Robustness and Controls}
\label{sec:robustness}

The result in Section~\ref{sec:results} is small, so it deserves more scrutiny than a
large one would. This section lists the checks that could have overturned it and what
each returned.

\paragraph{Paired comparison.}
All policies see the same episodes, the same merchant arrival order, and the same
failure penalty. The bootstrap resamples whole episode pairs rather than independent
draws per policy. An apparent ranking that came from which episodes were sampled
would not survive this, and the interval still excludes zero.

\paragraph{Honestly tuned baselines.}
Search depth and price threshold for the fixed rules are selected on calibration
episodes and then frozen. Had we tuned them on the evaluation panels, the fixed rules
would look better than any deployable version of themselves; had we left them
untuned, the adaptive rule would win trivially. The reported comparison is against
the strongest fixed rule available under the same information.

\paragraph{An offline bound.}
The exhaustive oracle inspects every merchant in the panel, including those an online
policy never queried, and is scored offline. It is unattainable by construction and
present to bound the achievable range. No online policy beats it in any episode; this
is asserted as a test rather than assumed.

\paragraph{A control that should fail.}
The \texttt{never accept} policy is scored under the same rules as the others. If
terminal failure were mishandled, declining everything would look attractive; it does
not, by a wide margin.

\paragraph{Budget enforcement.}
Every replayed episode is checked for hard-budget violations, of which there were
\ResultHardBudgetViolations{}. Because permits are reserved in full before dispatch
and censored usage is charged at the permit, overrun is prevented rather than
detected after the fact.

\paragraph{Solver agreement.}
The stopping solver is compared against exhaustive enumeration over all deterministic
policies on \ResultSolverCasesVerified{} small instances, in exact arithmetic. A
discrepancy would indicate that the recurrence, not the data, produced the result.
The values agree exactly.

\paragraph{Measurement reproducibility.}
Two full scans minutes apart returned byte-identical output. Any drift attributed to
the passage of time is therefore not scanner nondeterminism.

\subsection{What we could not check}

Two intended controls are not supported by the collected corpus and we record them as
unresolved rather than passed.

A \emph{nonbinding-budget twin}, which reruns the identical policy with budgets large
enough never to bind in order to separate the cost of the constraint from routing
error, is only meaningful when budgets actually bind. With a median of
\ResultMedianMerchantCount{} merchants per episode, the realistic budget already
covers the whole panel in most episodes, so the twin is nearly the same run.

A \emph{routing ablation} separating gains from choosing \emph{which} merchant to
query from gains from choosing \emph{when} to stop requires episodes with enough
merchants for order to matter. This corpus does not provide them. The live result
should be read as evidence about stopping alone, with routing untested.
